Levels and Linear Thinking

One page. Print it and keep it by the incident review.

Three levels (Meadows, ch. 4)

Level Question it answers Typical artifact Lets you
Event What just happened? Alert, ticket, tech log entry React
Behavior over time What keeps happening? Time-series graph, repeat-defect report Anticipate
Structure What produces it? Stocks, flows, loops, rules, information flows Redesign

Test: if your explanation would still hold after swapping every person involved, it's probably structural.

Four linear-thinking assumptions

  1. One cause.
  2. Effect close to cause in time and space.
  3. The fix doesn't change the system.
  4. Other actors won't react.

When an exercise answer relies on any of these, name it as a trap.

Key terms

Policy resistance Interventions defeated by the system's response to them (Sterman 2001).
"Side effects" Effects we didn't model: "no side effects—just effects" (Sterman 2006).

Senge's laws used most

From the eleven in The Fifth Discipline:

  • 1 Today's problems come from yesterday's "solutions."
  • 2 The harder you push, the harder the system pushes back.
  • 7 Cause and effect are not closely related in time and space.
  • 11 There is no blame.
