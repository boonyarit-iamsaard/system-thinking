# Systems Thinking Resources

## Knowledge

- [Book: _Thinking in Systems: A Primer_ by Donella H. Meadows (Chelsea Green, 2008; ISBN 978-1603580557)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thinking_In_Systems:_A_Primer)
  The canonical text for this course; its vocabulary and definitions win ties. Use for: stocks and flows, loops, delays, "why systems surprise us" (ch. 4), system traps, leverage points.
- [Essay: "Leverage Points: Places to Intervene in a System" by Donella Meadows (Donella Meadows Project)](https://donellameadows.org/archives/leverage-points-places-to-intervene-in-a-system/)
  Free full text of the 12 leverage points, ordered least to most effective. Use for: Module 4 and every "where does this intervention sit?" exercise.
- [Book: _The Fifth Discipline_ by Peter M. Senge (Doubleday/Currency, revised 2006; ISBN 978-0385517256)](https://search.worldcat.org/oclc/65166960)
  The eleven "laws of the fifth discipline" and the archetypes (fixes that fail, shifting the burden, limits to growth). Use for: Module 2 heuristics and Module 5 archetype diagrams. Weaker on stock/flow rigor than Meadows or Sterman.
- [Book: _Business Dynamics: Systems Thinking and Modeling for a Complex World_ by John D. Sterman (McGraw-Hill, 2000; ISBN 978-0072311358)](https://www.abebooks.com/9780072311358/Business-Dynamics-Systems-Thinking-Modeling-0072311355/plp)
  The rigorous reference for stock/flow accounting, delays and causal-loop conventions. Use for: checking polarities, delay behavior, and anything Meadows treats informally.
- [Paper: "System Dynamics Modeling: Tools for Learning in a Complex World" by John D. Sterman (_California Management Review_ 43(4), 2001)](https://faculty.sites.iastate.edu/tesfatsi/archive/tesfatsi/SystemDynamics.JohnSterman2001.pdf)
  Free, readable account of policy resistance and why intuition fails in dynamic systems. Use for: Module 1–2 framing; a short alternative to the textbook's chapter 1.
- [Paper: "Learning from Evidence in a Complex World" by John D. Sterman (_American Journal of Public Health_ 96(3), 2006)](https://www.mit.edu/~jsterman/Learning_from_Evidence.html)
  Why interventions generate unintended "side effects" and how feedback defeats policy. Use for: the "no side effects, just effects" argument.
- [Paper: "Bathtub Dynamics: Initial Results of a Systems Thinking Inventory" by Booth Sweeney & Sterman (_System Dynamics Review_ 16(4), 2000)](https://mitmgmtfaculty.mit.edu/jsterman/bathtubdynamics/)
  Evidence that even highly educated people misjudge stocks, flows and delays. Use for: Module 3; the bathtub tasks double as practice exercises.
- [Primer: _Introduction to Systems Thinking_ by Daniel H. Kim (Pegasus Communications)](https://embeddingproject.org/resources/introduction-to-systems-thinking/)
  Short practitioner primer, commonly cited for the Iceberg model and CLD notation. Use for: Module 2 Iceberg. Note: the Iceberg's origin is contested (Innovation Associates, Kim, Senge are all credited); it is not Meadows' framework.
- Course: Skooldio, "Systems Thinking for Better Problem Solving" (Thai, ~2h video)
  Sets the module sequence only; content not available to the agent. Could not be found by web search, so no link.

## Wisdom (Communities)

- [Learning from Incidents (LFI)](https://www.learningfromincidents.io/)
  Software-reliability practitioners applying systems and resilience thinking to real incidents; blog, newsletter, forums. Use for: testing Iceberg and structure-level incident write-ups against people who do this for a living.
- [System Dynamics Society](https://systemdynamics.org/membership/)
  The professional body for system dynamics (~1.4K members, 70 countries); webinars and an annual conference. Use for: critique of stock/flow and CLD models once past the basics.
